## Durability and GS-062

The six five-sample sets yielded **10/30 correct**. Of the 20 misses, 16 ended at semantic review, two at local completeness, one at terminal drafting timeout and one at the delivered-answer judge. The most likely shared cause of weak durability is a mismatch between the scope of the generated reply and the scope enforced at delivery. On repeated evidence the drafts drop required steps, or expand their advice beyond the cited branch. The same omitted Minecraft reopen step affected GS-023 and GS-030. Unsupported extra advice affected GS-018, GS-062 and GS-094, through different claims. Two reviews also rejected statements of uncertainty on GS-023 and GS-062. Frozen replays can determine whether those rejections are reproducible with the same text and citations. The GS-023 blind rescore independently shows scoring variability at the rejoin point. [Per-sample blockers and blind replay](kb-rerun24-diagnosis-evidence.md#durability-evidence)

**GS-062 regressed in observed delivery from 1/5 to 0/5.** The one R23 success explicitly distinguished general support from after-sales, kept the advice specific to the issue, and passed in 33.500 s. Every R24 attempt received the same eight ordered evidence spans as R23. R24's prefetch guidance made the distinction conditional on being asked, while the non-installer local check still requires it. All five R24 drafts omitted it. With passing reviews in samples 4 and 5, the local check was the direct blocker. In an offline check, citing a distinction scoped to the reported issue moved all five local flags to true. R24's three earlier reviewer failures would still need to clear review. The changed instructions are a plausible contributor to the repeated omission. The small, differently reviewed R23 sample leaves the size of the prompt effect unmeasured. [GS-062 traces and offline replay](kb-rerun24-diagnosis-evidence.md#durability-evidence)

## Fast and batch concurrency

The pinned Luna max probe sent `fast` and returned `default`. All 53 scheduled drafts with tier metadata also returned `default`. The local gateway source can normalize `fast` to `priority` and can filter it. The effective deployed rule, selected account, forwarded tier and upstream response tier remain unknown. The R24 benchmark was skipped. Confirmation requires the ingress tier, effective rule and account, egress request tier, upstream effective tier and returned client tier correlated to one request. An upstream response or usage record reporting priority establishes the upstream tier and a corresponding returned priority or fast tier closes the client leg. A fast response time is insufficient evidence. [Fast probe and guard](kb-rerun24-diagnosis-evidence.md#model-route-and-fast)

## Ranked follow-up targets and verification criteria

1. **Improve grounding and procedure coverage.** Prioritize GS-023's explicit reopen and expected conditional result, GS-018's transfer test and compatibility order, and GS-094's backup qualifier. Include admission of the upload and lost auto-installed JAR procedures where applicable, and test GS-163's repaired text against the activation notice. Require at least 4/13 correct deliveries and zero unscored in the same clean set as an interim benchmark. Require cited positive controls for version and transfer procedures to pass, all five negative controls to fail for their intended reasons, and a safe clean GS-056 handoff.
2. **Make correct deliveries repeatable.** Resolve GS-062's prompt, reviewer and local-check scope with a cited distinction specific to the reported problem. Require five of five correct cited deliveries for GS-018, GS-023, GS-030, GS-062 and GS-094. Run frozen cases through the gate and the same judge rubric, and record point-level differences on the five GS-023 blind controls. For an effort comparison, use paired, order-balanced GS-030 blocks with the same procedure and correlated route. Require better first-attempt correctness or fewer measured cutoffs with equivalent delivery before proposing an effort change to the owner.
3. **Recover GS-140 and the long tail.** Require five first-attempt correct trigger-on deliveries with both policy articles, explicit wait for technical staff in the existing ticket, after-sales and no-speedup guidance, and installer checks assigned to staff. Keep the explicit live-state handoff control. Require zero draft and review timeouts across the scheduled items and a 5/5 fix-4 corpus check. Record time by stage and use matched on/off samples to assess trigger cost within the 60 s turn.
4. **Verify the review and judge routes.** Correlate a verifier request and a judge request with gateway records containing the requested, mapped, sent and upstream response models and selected account. Capture client response model and IDs for both in the rerun and preserve the rubric and verdicts. Require zero verifier HTTP 400s and five correct fix-4 deliveries on the measured route. Use the fixed GS-023 judge set to check comparability before attributing a score change to a model.
5. **Confirm Fast before benchmarking width.** Require one pinned Luna max request with correlated evidence of Fast ingress, policy and account selection, priority egress, and priority or Fast upstream and client response tiers. Then compare matched serial and three-way blocks. Require lower wall time in both pairs, with equal or better correct delivery and no increase in timeouts or HTTP 429s.
